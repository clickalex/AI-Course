AGI: benchmarks, paths & safety

Everyone says “AGI is coming” — few say what would count as arriving. Learn the working definitions, the benchmarks that actually test generality, the competing paths to get there, and why safety work runs alongside.

Figure 47.1 — AGI isn’t one threshold; it’s a ladder of generality, autonomy, and reliability. Benchmarks are the rungs.

1. What “AGI” even means

LevelMeaningExample
ANINarrow: one task, superhuman or notChess engines, spam filters, translators
AGI (working def)Human-level across most cognitive tasks, incl. novel onesNot yet — closest: frontier LLMs + tools on some suites
ASIFar beyond best humans at nearly everythingSpeculative; the safety debate’s focus

Useful sharper framings: autonomy levels (copilot → agent → fully autonomous coworker) and generality + sample efficiency — doing new tasks from few examples, not regurgitating training.

2. Benchmarks that matter

Diagram 47.1 — The ladder: memorisable knowledge at the bottom, genuine novel-task autonomy at the top. Climbing needs reasoning + agency, not just parameters.
  • ARC-AGI: abstract puzzles designed to be unsolvable by memorisation — the purest generality test.
  • SWE-bench / terminal-bench: do real work (fix code, use tools) judged by outcomes, not vibes.
  • METR-style autonomy evals: how long a task (minutes → hours → days) can an agent complete end-to-end?
  • Contamination caveat: any public benchmark leaks into training eventually — private/hidden tests + novel tasks are the only durable signal (Ch 46).

3. Competing paths (and what each bets on)

  • Scale: bigger models + data + compute (scaling laws). Bet: generality emerges. Risk: data walls, cost, reasoning plateaus.
  • Reasoning + test-time compute: think longer, verify, search (Ch 49). Bet: inference-time effort beats parameters for hard tasks.
  • Agents + tools + memory: scaffold models into systems (Ch 23–24, 42). Bet: composition > monolith.
  • Embodiment + world models: learn physics by acting (robotics, VLA). Bet: grounding cures hallucination.
  • Neuro-symbolic / new paradigms: verifiable reasoning cores. Bet: guarantees matter more than fluency.

Why safety is part of the chapter, not an appendix

More capable + more autonomous = higher stakes for misspecification (reward hacking, Ch 15), misuse, and concentration of power. The working consensus: evals for dangerous capabilities, alignment research, staged deployment, and governance — capability and safety are one conversation (Ch 28).

Short notes

AGI = human-level across most cognitive tasks incl. novel ones; measure with ARC-AGI, SWE-bench, and hours-long autonomy evals — watching for contamination. Paths: scale, test-time reasoning, agents, embodiment, neuro-symbolic. Pair every capability claim with evals, alignment, and staged deployment.

Practice questions

P1. A lab claims “AGI achieved” after topping MMLU. Your 3-sentence rebuttal + the eval you’d demand.

MMLU is largely retrievable knowledge and likely contaminated — topping it shows memory, not generality. Demand: ARC-AGI-style novel tasks plus hidden, outcome-judged autonomy evals (hours-long, tool-using, unmemorisable). One saturated exam ≠ human-level across novel cognitive work.

Self-mark:

P2. Sketch an autonomy eval for “plan + book a 3-day trip under ₹30k.” What makes it hard?

Agent must search flights/hotels, compare under budget, handle constraints (visa, timings), book via tools, and produce an itinerary + receipts. Hard parts: multi-step tool use, constraint tracking, error recovery (sold out → replan), real side effects needing approval gates, and judging quality beyond “it booked something.” Score: constraints met + cost + robustness.

Self-mark:

P3. Argue both sides: “AGI by 2030” vs “not in our lifetimes.”

Bull: scaling + reasoning + agents compounding; autonomy horizons doubling yearly; economics funds it. Bear: reasoning plateaus, data walls, embodiment unsolved, evals keep exposing brittleness; past AI timelines humbled everyone. Honest position: track autonomy-horizon metrics, not vibes — update yearly.
