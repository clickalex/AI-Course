Chapter 22 · LLMs in practice

Fine-tuning, PEFT & evals

You rarely train a foundation model. You adapt one — cheaply — and you prove it with evals, not vibes.

Figure 22.1 — Freeze the giant model; train a cheap, swappable adapter.

  1. Prompt / few-shot — no weight change.
  2. SFT — supervised instruction data; full or LoRA.
  3. PEFT — LoRA/QLoRA, adapters, prefix-tuning: train a tiny residual, freeze the rest.
  4. Preference — DPO / RLHF / ORPO so style and safety match raters.
  5. RAG — don’t memorise facts; fetch them (often better than fine-tunes for changing knowledge).
LoRA: W' = W + BAB, A low-rank; W frozen. QLoRA stores W in 4-bit.

Evals: held-out task suites, factuality with citations, toxicity, prompt-injection tests, cost/latency, and human rubrics. Overfitting a famous public benchmark is the new train/test leak.

Eval anti-patterns that still ship

Testing only on in-distribution happy-path prompts; reporting one aggregate score that hides broken language/topic slices; no frozen regression set as prompts drift; “LLM-as-judge” without a written rubric or calibration against humans; ignoring over-refusal and unanswerable-question handling; no tokens-per-task cost, latency or injection tests. Ship when the golden set, adversarial set, human rubric and canary metrics all agree.

Short notes

Choose the cheapest adapter that hits the metric. Fine-tune for style and format; retrieve for facts. Quantisation (INT8/4) is how 7B models fit on a laptop GPU.

Point notes

  • LoRA = low-rank update.
  • DPO ≈ RLHF without a separate PPO loop.
  • Eval set ≠ the tweet demo.
  • RAG vs FT: freshness vs behaviour.

Facts that change belong in the index.

Direct Preference Optimization.

Benchmark overfitting (test-set leakage) makes public numbers diverge from product quality.

P1. You have 400 high-quality support transcripts. Full FT or LoRA? Why?

LoRA/QLoRA: small data, want to keep general skills, cheap to iterate. Full FT on 400 examples will overfit and catastrophic-forget. Add a style eval and a factual RAG layer for product names.

Self-mark:

P2. Give one scenario where RAG is the right choice and one where fine-tuning is, and say why.

RAG: a policy assistant whose knowledge changes weekly and needs citations and deletions — retrieval over the live corpus gives freshness and provenance without retraining. Fine-tuning (LoRA): a stable task defined by behaviour, tone or format (always emitting a particular JSON schema, mimicking a brand voice, a fixed extraction task) — adapters bake in style cheaply. Production often combines both: retrieve for facts, adapt for behaviour.

Self-mark:

P3. Your model beats the offline eval after fine-tuning, but users complain. Name three likely eval gaps.

(1) The offline set is too easy or in-distribution, while real traffic contains noise, long inputs and new intents; (2) no adversarial/prompt-injection or refusal edge-case suite and no per-language/per-slice analysis; (3) success was measured on answer similarity rather than end-to-end task completion, with latency/cost and human-rubric scores ignored. Add a frozen golden set, traffic-shadowed hard cases, human review and canary metrics.
