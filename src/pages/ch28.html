Chapter 28 · PhD / safety

XAI, alignment, adversarial ML & privacy

If it matters, you must explain it, attack it, and stop it leaking. Alignment is the long-term version of “it does what we meant.”

Figure 28.1 — Interpretability opens the box; alignment aims the gears at intended goals.

Explainability

SHAP/LIME are local attributions — useful, lie-able. Saliency maps on images can look convincing and be independent of the label. Mechanistic interpretability tries to reverse-engineer circuits inside nets (PhD-hot). For products, prefer inherently simpler models or structured audits over a heatmap theatre.

Adversarial ML

Tiny perturbations flip classifiers (FGSM, PGD). Poisoning and backdoors hit training. Jailbreaks hit LLMs. Defence is hard; don’t claim “robust” without the attack model.

Privacy

Membership inference, training-data extraction from LLMs. Mitigations: data minimisation, differential privacy (DP-SGD), never train on secrets.

Alignment

Specification gaming, inner vs outer alignment, scalable oversight, debate, constitutional AI, evals for dangerous capabilities. This is research plus ops — not a checkbox.

Three time-scales of safety work

Now: bias, privacy, security and misuse, increasingly covered by regulation (EU AI Act risk tiers; emerging Indian AI governance frameworks). Emerging: oversight of highly autonomous agents in cybersecurity, coding and science. Long-term: aligning systems that may outperform their overseers. All three share a method — name the threat model, red-team before deployment, and keep humans able to intervene.

Short notes

Explanations can be fake. Attacks exist. Privacy is a threat model. Alignment is making highly capable systems pursue intended goals under distribution shift and deception pressure.

Point notes

  • SHAP ≠ causal truth.
  • Adversarial examples are not just noise.
  • DP: add calibrated noise to gradients/queries.
  • Jailbreak = attack on an aligned policy.

P1. A bank shows a SHAP plot to a denied applicant. What must you still say?

SHAP is a local attribution under a model of missingness, not a legal or causal “because.” Correlated features share credit arbitrarily. For adverse action, prefer monotonic simple models or constrained policy rules, plus human review — not only a waterfall chart.

P2. Explain the privacy–utility trade-off in DP-SGD.

DP-SGD clips each example's gradient (bounding one person's influence) then adds Gaussian noise scaled to the clipping norm and privacy budget; repeating queries accumulates budget, tracked via composition (ε, δ). Smaller ε means stronger guarantees but more noise and lower accuracy — privacy is literally purchased with utility. Combine with data minimisation, sampling and accountants; report ε like any other hyperparameter.

Self-mark:

P3. A paper shows beautiful saliency maps for a medical classifier. Give two concrete reasons for skepticism.

(1) Saliency/gradient maps can be insensitive to the model — near-identical maps appear for randomly initialised or label-shuffled networks, and different XAI methods disagree on the same image; they visualise gradient sensitivity, not causal reasons. (2) They are unaudited: validate with insertion/deletion curves, counterfactual examples, perturbation tests and clinician-grounded evaluation. For high-stakes decisions prefer simpler constrained models plus human review.
