Advanced RL, MARL & world models

Beyond toy Q-learning: partial observability, many agents, learning a simulator, and using RL on language policies.

Figure 25.1 — Dream first: plan and rehearse inside a learned model of the world.

  • POMDP — you don’t see s, only observations; need memory (RNN, belief state).
  • Offline RL — learn from a log without more env interaction (conservative Q, IQL). Easy to overestimate unseen actions.
  • Model-based / world models — learn T(s′|s,a), plan inside the dream (Dreamer, MuZero).
  • MARL — non-stationarity: other learners change the env. CTDE (centralised training, decentralised exec), Nash, mean-field.
  • Imitation — behaviour cloning; DAgger when you can query an expert.
  • LLM+RL — RLHF, process rewards, RL on verifiable tasks (math, code tests).

Q-learning updateQ(s,a) ← Q(s,a) + α [ r + γ maxₐ′ Q(s′,a′) − Q(s,a) ] — the bracketed term is the temporal-difference error: reality plus discounted future value minus the old prediction. α is step size, γ is patience for delayed reward.

Before reaching for RL, ask three questions: is there a reward you can trust, can you interact cheaply and safely, and is the problem genuinely sequential? Two “no” answers usually mean supervised learning or a bandit is the right tool.

Short notes

Most “RL for products” still dies on reward design and sample cost. World models and offline logs help. Multi-agent is a different theory. For LLMs, prefer verifiable rewards over vague preference when you can.

Point notes

  • POMDP = hidden state.
  • Offline RL ≠ copy the log blindly.
  • MARL: opponents learn too.
  • World model = learned simulator.

MCQs

5 questions · scored

1. A POMDP differs from an MDP because:

Partial observability.

2. In MARL, non-stationarity means:

Your data from yesterday’s opponents is stale.

3. A world model is:

Model-based RL.

4. Behaviour cloning is:

It is the simplest imitation method; covariate shift is its classic failure, fixed partly by DAgger.

5. Conservative Q-learning and similar offline RL methods deliberately:

Without pessimism the agent extrapolates to falsely wonderful actions no log ever tried.

P1. Why is “RL on an LLM with a learned reward model” fragile compared with “RL on unit-test pass rate”?

Reward models can be gamed (sycophancy, verbose fluff). Unit tests are a ground-truth verifier. Prefer verifiable rewards when the domain allows; keep humans for taste and safety that tests miss.

Self-mark:

P2. Why can self-play collapse or cycle, and how do league-based systems counter it?

Agents optimised against a single opponent overfit its strategy or enter rock–paper–scissors-style cycles as yesterday's counter-strategy becomes today's target — the environment is non-stationary. Maintaining a population/pool of past and diverse opponents (fictitious self-play, league training, PFSP), sampling partners and adversaries, and aiming for Nash-style robustness prevents collapse to a single exploitable style.

Self-mark:

P3. When is behaviour cloning enough, and when must you add RL? How does DAgger help?

BC suffices when high-quality demonstrations densely cover the states the system will meet and the task is directly imitable. Add RL when no demos exist, the policy must exceed the demonstrator, or a verifiable reward exists (games, unit tests). BC compounds small errors into off-distribution states it never saw; DAgger queries the expert on states the learner actually visits and adds them to training, closing that covariate-shift gap.
